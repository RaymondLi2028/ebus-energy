01 · THE PROJECT

From electrification to optimization.

The transition to electric school buses creates a second question: how should those buses be charged?

In September 2025, the Bay Area Air District announced a $1.18 million grant to SRVUSD to replace five compressed-natural-gas buses and two diesel buses with seven zero-emission electric school buses, together with charging infrastructure.

This independent student project asks what comes next: how charging schedules, renewable electricity, operational needs, and future grid interaction could be analyzed to improve environmental and economic outcomes.

This website distinguishes confirmed public information from research questions and future scenarios. No operational data is assumed until it is obtained and verified.

CORE RESEARCH QUESTION

How can SRVUSD optimize electric school bus charging to increase renewable-energy use while reducing electricity costs, peak demand, and carbon emissions—without compromising transportation reliability?
02 · RESEARCH PILLARS

Four connected areas of study.

01

Smart Charging

Compare immediate, scheduled, and load-managed charging strategies against route and vehicle availability.

02

Renewable Integration

Study whether charging demand can be better aligned with periods of cleaner or locally generated electricity.

03

Environmental Impact

Develop a transparent framework to estimate energy use, avoided tailpipe emissions, and carbon impacts.

04

Future V2G Potential

Explore the technical and operational conditions under which parked buses might eventually provide bidirectional grid services.

03 · METHOD

A project built around evidence—not assumptions.

The analysis will become more specific as verified data and expert input become available.

  1. Establish the baseline.Review public SRVUSD, Air District, utility, and technology-provider materials.
  2. Engage stakeholders.Seek interviews with SRVUSD transportation/facilities staff and relevant charging or fleet experts.
  3. Collect appropriate data.Request non-sensitive route, charging, energy, equipment, and scheduling information where available.
  4. Model scenarios.Compare charging strategies using clearly documented assumptions and reproducible calculations.
  5. Evaluate tradeoffs.Assess reliability, energy use, emissions, cost, peak demand, and future V2G feasibility.
  6. Share findings.Publish a student research report, visual dashboard, and concise recommendations.

Student research at the intersection of science, energy, and public policy.
