The transition to electric school buses creates a second question: how should those buses be charged? This project begins with SRVUSD as an initial case study and aims to examine the challenges and opportunities for electric school bus charging across California public school districts. In September 2025, the Bay Area Air District announced a $1.18 million grant to SRVUSD to replace five compressed-natural-gas buses and two diesel buses with seven zero-emission electric school buses, together with charging infrastructure. Using SRVUSD as a starting point, this research asks how charging schedules, renewable electricity, operational needs, and future grid interaction might affect environmental and economic outcomes in different California districts. Local findings will not automatically be generalized statewide. This website distinguishes confirmed public information from research questions and future scenarios. No operational data is assumed until it is obtained and verified. CORE RESEARCH QUESTION How can California public school districts optimize electric school bus charging to increase renewable-energy use while reducing electricity costs, peak demand, and carbon emissions—without compromising transportation reliability? SRVUSD serves as the initial case study. 02 · RESEARCH PILLARS Four connected areas of study. 01 Smart Charging Compare immediate, scheduled, and load-managed charging strategies against route and vehicle availability. 02 Renewable Integration Study whether charging demand can be better aligned with periods of cleaner or locally generated electricity. 03 Environmental Impact Develop a transparent framework to estimate energy use, avoided tailpipe emissions, and carbon impacts. 04 Future V2G Potential Explore the technical and operational conditions under which parked buses might eventually provide bidirectional grid services. 03 · METHOD A project built around evidence—not assumptions. The analysis will become more specific as verified data and expert input become available. 1 Establish the baseline.Begin with public SRVUSD and Air District materials; expand to California school district, state policy, utility, and technology-provider sources. 2 Engage stakeholders.Seek interviews with SRVUSD transportation/facilities staff and relevant experts; later expand outreach to other California districts. 3 Collect appropriate data.Request non-sensitive route, charging, energy, equipment, and scheduling information where available. 4 Model scenarios.Compare charging scenarios using documented assumptions, reproducible calculations, and regional considerations relevant to California. 5 Evaluate tradeoffs.Assess reliability, energy use, emissions, cost, peak demand, and future V2G feasibility. 6 Share findings.Publish a student research report, visual dashboard, and concise recommendations. 04 · PROJECT ROADMAP Research in progress.
